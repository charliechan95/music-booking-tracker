# Friend validation checklist — booking tracker

Charlie: use this when you talk to your friend. Goal is NOT to demo a finished
product — it's to learn how he actually works, so the tracker gets fitted to
his real process. That fitting work is what turns a free build into paid
services work.

## Frame it as a prototype

One line to open with: "I had a rough prototype built — before I tune it for
you, tell me how you actually handle bookings today." Keep it rough, keep it
his.

## 10 questions (pick 5–6, his patience is finite)

1. How do gigs reach you today — agent, referrals, WhatsApp groups, cold venues?
2. Where do you keep track right now — spreadsheet, notebook, phone notes, memory?
3. Walk me through your last booking from first contact to show day. Where did it almost fall apart?
4. What info do you need per gig? (fee, date, contact, travel/visa, soundcheck, stage plot?)
5. How do follow-ups work — how do you remember to chase a venue that went quiet?
6. How often does a lead go cold because you forgot to follow up?
7. Do you track money owed / received per gig anywhere?
8. Do you work with a manager or agent who'd also need access?
9. Phone or laptop — which does he actually use on the road?
10. What's the single most annoying part of the whole process?

## What to listen for

- If he says "spreadsheet": ask what columns. Match them, don't argue.
- If he says "memory": the overdue follow-up alert is your selling point.
- If he says "agent handles it": the client may be the agent, not him.
- Anything he mentions twice = the feature that matters.

## After the call

Bring his answers back. The tracker gets one tuning pass against his real
workflow before anyone calls it done.
